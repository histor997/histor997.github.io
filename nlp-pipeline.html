Jesuit Corpus Explorer

Part of Mapping Classical Reception in Beijing. A prototype for asking how Jesuit missionaries describe themselves in private petitions and in letters edited for publication.

What this page shows

18 scored records from the Lettres édifiantes (volumes 9 to 13), rated on seven dimensions of self-description. Scores are shown per 1,000 words, so long and short letters can be compared.

The spread between published letters, which is the baseline any genre difference has to beat.

What it does not show yet

The private side. The comparison needs Latin Indipetae written by the same authors, which are held in the Jesuit archives in Rome and are not yet in my hands.

So there is no genre gap on this page, and nothing here should be read as one.

3. The comparison this is built for

The question is whether one author's private petition and his published letters differ by more than published letters differ from each other. The design has three steps.

  1. Pair each author with his own Indipeta. The comparison has to be same-author, same-era. Ricci, who died in 1610, never appears in the Lettres, which began in 1702. His published counterpart would be Trigault's 1615 edition of his journals, a different kind of text.
  2. Score both texts the same way and divide by length. A short petition and a long letter are only comparable per 1,000 words, and a very short sample is unstable in either direction.
  3. Compare the gap to the spread in section 1. If the genre gap is no larger than the difference between two published letters, it is not evidence of editing.

4. Notes on method

Scores come from the research pipeline behind this project, which combines dictionary matching, embedding similarity, and LLM annotation across seven dimensions. This page displays results and does not run the pipeline.

In the product spec for a museum-kiosk version, one rule was fixed before any interface work: any passage the model cites must appear word for word in the source text, or it is dropped before display. A fabricated quotation next to real Latin is the one error a specialist would catch at once.

Data caveats. Scores are word-list counts and depend on the dictionaries used. The scientific-self axis is almost empty in this corpus (highest value 0.15 per 1,000 words). Two author-volume pairs, Cholenec in volume 12 and Martin in volume 13, appear as two records each with different word counts. I have kept them separate, marked (a) and (b), until I can check whether they overlap.
